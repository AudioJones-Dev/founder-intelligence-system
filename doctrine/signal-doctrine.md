# Signal Doctrine

## Core Thesis
Strategic quality is constrained by signal quality. Decisions should be grounded in explicit, testable, and context-aware signals rather than narrative convenience.

## Strategic Interpretation
When evidence is ambiguous, prioritize falsifiable interpretation paths. Preserve minority signals when they imply high downside risk.

## Foundational Assumptions
- Not all signals deserve equal interpretive weight.
- Fast decisions require explicit confidence and error tolerance.
- Organizational memory must preserve both outcomes and reasoning.

## Relationship To Other Frameworks
This doctrine governs how Signal Audit Framework and Founder Prediction Error Model interpret evidence and update assumptions.

## Business Implications
- Better capital allocation discipline
- Faster correction of strategic drift
- Higher reliability in cross-functional decision alignment

## AI Implications
Agents must justify signal selection, expose uncertainty, and avoid collapsing contradictory evidence into a single narrative.

## Failure Modes
- Doctrine treated as rigid dogma instead of interpretive constraint
- Over-indexing on measurable signals while missing structural shifts
- Excessive skepticism that blocks timely execution

## Compression Summary
Use explicit signal quality and falsifiability as the default foundation for strategic decisions.
