# Simplicity Arbitrage (Doctrine)

## Core Thesis
In feature-saturated categories, simplicity is positional advantage. The buyer rewards whoever names the outcome most clearly and delivers it with the fewest steps. Complexity is not depth — it is the residue of a vendor team that has not done the work of compression.

## Strategic Interpretation
Every mature category drifts in the same direction: vendor language eclipses buyer language, pricing pages require calculators, demos take an hour, and comparison sites exist primarily to translate the category back into something the buyer can use. This drift is an arbitrage. Whoever speaks the buyer's job back to them in plain language captures the next cycle of buying attention.

This doctrine is the *worldview* behind `frameworks/simplicity-arbitrage.md`. The framework is the diagnostic; this is the belief that makes the diagnostic worth running.

## Foundational Assumptions
- Buyers default to the language that maps to their actual job. Vendor language drifts away from that over time.
- Categories accumulate complexity the way machines accumulate cruft — through accretion, not design.
- Feature lists are evidence of a product team's anxiety about saying what it does, not of customer value.
- The cost of comprehension is paid by the buyer in attention. The cost of clarity is paid by the vendor in discipline. Most vendors push the cost onto the buyer.

## Business Implications
- Name the outcome before listing the features.
- Pricing pages are the most undervalued surface. They should be readable in 10 seconds.
- The CEO should write the homepage. Marketing teams that inherit category language perpetuate it.

## AI Implications
- Agents that explain instead of decide push complexity back to the user.
- The premium agent surfaces say *do X* rather than *here are 12 options*.

## Failure Modes
- Stripping features without simplifying positioning. The product looks lean; the messaging is still drowned.
- Simplifying language while leaving complex pricing — undoing the move at the moment of conversion.
- Calling minimalism a moat when it is merely an absence.

## Falsifiers
- Feature-heavy competitors consistently outperform simpler competitors in mature categories
- Buyers reliably reward complexity of message over clarity of outcome

## Compression Summary
**The market punishes complexity it didn't ask for. Whoever names the outcome cleanest takes the cycle.**
