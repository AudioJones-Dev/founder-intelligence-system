# N.I.C.H.E. Framework

> **Maturity:** draft / evolving. This framework is currently less mature than Signal Doctrine, M.A.P. Attribution, Signal Audit, and Founder Prediction Error. The five letters and the scoring rubric should be expected to iterate. Use it, but flag findings explicitly as preliminary.

## Purpose
Identify defensible market opportunities by finding where five things overlap: hidden demand, operational inefficiency, unique capability, sharp messaging, and strong economics. A niche is not a small audience. A niche is a **high-signal market gap**.

## Problem It Solves
Founders routinely conflate "niche" with "small audience" or "narrow segment." That definition produces small unprofitable bets and stalls growth. N.I.C.H.E. forces a five-dimensional scoring pass that distinguishes a genuine market gap from a vanity narrow.

## Core Components

### N — Non-Obvious Demand
Find the demand others miss.

**Operational questions:**
- What pain is under-discussed in this market?
- What urgent problem is hidden in plain sight?
- What do prospects complain about but not search directly?
- What market behavior reveals unmet demand?

**Output:** A high-signal demand insight — named, sourced, and falsifiable.

### I — Inefficiency
Find the broken process.

**Operational questions:**
- Where is money leaking?
- Where is time being wasted?
- Where does the customer experience break?
- What manual process is begging to be systemized?

**Output:** A clear operational gap, quantified where possible.

### C — Capability
Match the opportunity to a unique capability.

**Operational questions:**
- What can we do better than the market?
- What skill stack gives us leverage?
- What technology, insight, or process can we deploy that competitors cannot easily replicate?
- What do we understand that others ignore?

**Output:** A capability-market fit statement.

### H — Hook
Compress the insight into a market-facing message.

**Operational questions:**
- What is the sharpest way to name the pain?
- What makes the prospect stop scrolling?
- What creates immediate recognition?
- What is the one-line reframe?

**Output:** A positioning hook that earns a meeting in one sentence.

### E — Economics
Validate the business model.

**Operational questions:**
- Can this produce revenue?
- Is the pain expensive enough?
- Can the buyer afford the solution?
- Can this be sold repeatedly?
- Can this scale into a system?

**Output:** A monetizable offer thesis with unit economics that survive scrutiny.

## Related Skills
- **Ghost Note Detection** — to surface demand that exists but isn't being named in the market
- **Signal Hierarchy Analysis** — to rank candidate niches by signal density
- **Attribution Analysis** — to validate that the proposed capability actually creates the proposed outcome

## Operating Logic
1. Run each letter in order; resist the temptation to design the hook before the demand
2. For each letter, output the diagnostic artifact (insight, gap, capability statement, hook, monetization thesis)
3. Score each dimension 1–10; the composite is **not** a simple average — a single zero in any dimension invalidates the niche
4. Stress-test by attempting to falsify each finding (Phase 6 of the framework)
5. If the niche survives, produce a one-page niche thesis suitable for offer design and category positioning

## Inputs
- Market observations (qualitative and quantitative)
- Capability inventory (what the team can demonstrably do better than competitors)
- Economic constraints (CAC ceiling, gross-margin floor, retention assumptions)
- Candidate buyer profile

## Outputs
- A niche thesis (one page, falsifiable)
- A positioning hook
- A monetization thesis with unit economics
- Named falsification conditions

## N.I.C.H.E. Diagnostic Table

| Letter | Layer | Output |
|---|---|---|
| N | Non-Obvious Demand | Hidden market pain |
| I | Inefficiency | Broken process |
| C | Capability | Unique leverage |
| H | Hook | Market-facing message |
| E | Economics | Monetization logic |

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Niche fails on at least one dimension; do not pursue |
| 3 | Niche has signal but at least one dimension is weak |
| 5 | All five dimensions present but unvalidated |
| 7 | Three or more dimensions validated against real buyers |
| 10 | All five dimensions validated against revenue across at least one cycle |

## Failure Modes
- Treating "small audience" as synonymous with "niche"
- Naming a niche the market does not search for or talk about
- Letting capability drive the niche without demand validation
- Mistaking a clever hook for a real positioning
- Skipping the economics layer — niches that can't be sold profitably are not niches

## Strategic Value
A niche that scores well on N.I.C.H.E. is a niche the team can stake a year of effort on without rewriting the thesis quarterly. The five-dimension structure resists the most common positioning failures.

## Example Applications
- Founder differentiation work
- Productized consulting offer design
- Service business category creation
- Local / vertical market selection
- Pre-launch positioning validation

## Compression Summary
**A niche is a high-signal market gap with demand, dysfunction, leverage, language, and economics. Lose one and it's a vanity narrow, not a niche.**
