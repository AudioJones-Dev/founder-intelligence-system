# Complexity Compression Theory

## Core Definition
A business gains strategic advantage when it reduces operational complexity while preserving or increasing leverage.

## Core Question
Where is complexity increasing faster than value?

## Diagnostic Signals
- too many offers
- too many tools
- too many handoffs
- founder remains bottleneck
- delivery depends on undocumented judgment
- AI increases output but not clarity

## Diagnostic Questions
- How many tools does a user touch to complete one job?
- How many decisions does a buyer make before purchase?
- How many handoffs exist between problem and outcome?
- Where does AI add output without adding clarity?
- What process exists only in the founder's head?

## Opportunity Pattern
When a market is overwhelmed by complexity, the winning company often creates the simplest path to the desired outcome.

## Use This Framework To Evaluate
- AI products
- service businesses
- internal operations
- founder-led companies
- SaaS opportunities
- consulting offers

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Low complexity problem |
| 3 | Moderate friction |
| 5 | Severe complexity drag |
| 7 | Complexity blocks growth |
| 10 | Complexity is the primary market pain |

## Scoring Dimensions
- **Workflow fragmentation** — how many discrete steps/tools/people
- **Cognitive load** — how many decisions the buyer or operator must make
- **Output-to-clarity ratio** — how much produced material vs decisions enabled
- **Founder dependency** — how much judgment is undocumented

## Strategic Output
If score is 7+, look for a productized simplification opportunity.

## Common Failure Modes
- Mistaking feature richness for value
- Adding AI on top of complexity instead of compressing it
- Treating complexity as a moat when it's actually drag
