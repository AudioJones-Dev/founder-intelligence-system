# Curse of Capability

## Core Definition
A capable founder builds a business that requires their capability — creating a ceiling disguised as a strength.

## Core Question
What is this business doing only because the founder *can*?

## Diagnostic Signals
- founder delivers the core work personally
- multiple offers exist because founder is good at multiple things
- "I'll just do it" pattern across functions
- no one else can replicate critical decisions
- pricing reflects founder's time, not productized value
- new hires get stuck without founder input

## Diagnostic Questions
- What would break if the founder went offline for 30 days?
- Which offers exist because of capability vs. demand?
- Where is judgment being applied that hasn't been documented?
- What part of the business *only* the founder can do?

## Opportunity Pattern
The curse of capability creates personal businesses that look successful but cannot scale, sell, or be inherited. The fix is to extract judgment into systems, narrow the offer, and remove the founder from delivery.

## Use This Framework To Evaluate
- agencies and consultancies
- expert-led service businesses
- solo SaaS founders wearing every hat
- creator businesses
- acquisition targets where the founder *is* the business

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Founder is fully removed from delivery |
| 3 | Founder involved in strategy only |
| 5 | Founder is a key approver |
| 7 | Founder is the primary deliverer |
| 10 | Business cannot function without the founder |

## Scoring Dimensions
- **Delivery dependency** — % of revenue requiring founder hands-on
- **Decision dependency** — % of decisions requiring founder approval
- **Offer sprawl** — number of distinct offers tied to founder skills
- **Knowledge externalization** — % of judgment captured in systems

## Strategic Output
If score is 7+, the business is a job, not an asset. Productization, judgment-extraction, and offer-narrowing are the primary unlock.

## Common Failure Modes
- Hiring help without removing the founder from the decision path
- Adding offers instead of deepening one
- Mistaking personal mastery for transferable IP
