# Simplicity Arbitrage

## Core Definition
In categories crowded with feature-heavy competitors, simpler positioning, simpler pricing, and simpler product surfaces capture disproportionate value.

## Core Question
Where is the market punishing complexity that customers didn't ask for?

## Diagnostic Signals
- competitors lead with feature lists
- pricing pages require a calculator
- onboarding takes more than one session
- buyers ask "which plan do I need?"
- category language drifts further from user language
- comparison sites exist primarily to decode the category

## Diagnostic Questions
- What does the customer actually want to say happened?
- Which features do buyers ignore in the first 30 days?
- What would a "one-button" version of this product look like?
- Where does the competitor's pricing confuse even their own sales team?

## Opportunity Pattern
Whoever names the outcome most clearly and delivers it with the fewest steps wins the next cycle. Simplicity is a positioning weapon, not a product limitation.

## Use This Framework To Evaluate
- SaaS in mature categories
- consulting offers
- AI products competing on capability
- consumer apps with feature bloat
- pricing redesigns

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Category is already simple |
| 3 | Mild complexity, low arbitrage |
| 5 | Real positioning opportunity |
| 7 | Category is choking on its own complexity |
| 10 | A clearly simpler offer would dominate |

## Scoring Dimensions
- **Positioning clarity gap** — how confused buyer language is vs vendor language
- **Pricing comprehension** — can a buyer understand pricing in 10 seconds
- **Onboarding compression** — time-to-first-value across category
- **Feature-to-outcome ratio** — features shipped vs outcomes delivered

## Strategic Output
If score is 7+, a positioning-and-product simplification play is on the table — often with a 2–3x price premium for clarity.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing minimalism with simplicity (stripping features instead of compressing decisions)
- Simplifying the surface while leaving complex pricing
- Naming the product simply but selling it with a complex demo
