# Time Recovery Economics

## Core Definition
The economic logic of products and services that *give the buyer their time back* — not by working faster, but by removing entire categories of work, decisions, or attention.

## Core Question
How many hours, decisions, and context-switches does this remove from the buyer's life?

## Diagnostic Signals
- competitors sell "save time" but the buyer's calendar doesn't change
- "productivity" tools that add notifications instead of removing them
- AI offers that produce more material the buyer must review
- pricing pegged to seats instead of hours recovered
- buyer cannot point to *which* hours were freed

## Diagnostic Questions
- Which hour in the buyer's week disappears because of this offer?
- Which recurring decisions go away entirely?
- Which meetings become unnecessary?
- Does the buyer's attention shrink or simply shift?

## Opportunity Pattern
In the AI era, time-saving is table stakes. Time-*recovery* — eliminating whole categories of work — is the premium offer. Buyers will pay non-linearly for hours they reliably get back.

## Use This Framework To Evaluate
- AI products
- executive assistants and ops services
- automation offers
- premium pricing strategies
- positioning against feature-heavy competitors

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Negligible time impact |
| 3 | Saves minutes, not hours |
| 5 | Saves hours but adds review work |
| 7 | Recovers hours cleanly, with low oversight |
| 10 | Removes entire categories of work from the buyer's life |

## Scoring Dimensions
- **Hours recovered** — total time returned per period
- **Decision relief** — number of recurring decisions eliminated
- **Oversight cost** — time required to supervise the solution
- **Reliability** — how consistently the recovery holds up

## Strategic Output
If score is 7+, price against the value of the recovered time, not the cost of the labor replaced. This is where premium pricing lives.

## Common Failure Modes
- Selling "time savings" that the buyer cannot feel
- Adding review work that quietly cancels the saving
- Pricing on cost-to-serve instead of value-recovered
