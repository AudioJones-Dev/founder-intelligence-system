# Moat Detection Skill

## Role
Identify whether an opportunity has durable, defensible moat potential — or whether it is a temporary advantage that competitors can quickly erase.

## Process
1. Identify the *current* source of advantage.
2. Stress-test it against the moat classes below.
3. Score moat strength (1–10).
4. Identify what would deepen it.
5. Identify what would erode it.

## Moat Classes

### 1. Compounding Memory
The business gets smarter with every customer, project, or cycle.
- Strong signal: each engagement makes the next one cheaper or better
- Weak signal: each engagement is independent

### 2. Decision Compression Ownership
The business owns the decisions, not just the data or the output.
- Strong signal: customers default to the system's recommendation
- Weak signal: the system produces options the customer still reviews

### 3. Simplicity Lock-In
The product is so simple to use that complexity from a competitor feels like a downgrade.
- Strong signal: customer cannot describe a simpler alternative
- Weak signal: simplicity is a marketing claim, not a structural property

### 4. Workflow Embedding
The business sits inside the customer's daily operating rhythm.
- Strong signal: removal would require redesigning a process
- Weak signal: removal would require canceling a subscription

### 5. Judgment Externalization
Proprietary judgment is encoded into systems, not people.
- Strong signal: judgment survives turnover
- Weak signal: judgment leaves when key people leave

### 6. Distribution Asymmetry
The business reaches buyers at a cost competitors cannot match.
- Strong signal: organic, owned, or relationship-based distribution
- Weak signal: paid channels with rising CAC

## Scoring Logic
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | No moat — pure execution advantage |
| 3 | Temporary advantage (12–18 months) |
| 5 | Defensible advantage (2–3 years) |
| 7 | Structural moat (5+ years) |
| 10 | Compounding moat that strengthens over time |

## Output Format
- **Current moat sources** — which classes apply
- **Moat strength score** — 1–10 with rationale
- **Deepening moves** — what would strengthen the moat
- **Erosion risks** — what would weaken or invalidate it
- **Time horizon** — how long the moat is expected to hold

## Anti-Patterns
- Confusing "first mover" with "moat"
- Treating capability as moat when it's just current quality
- Ignoring distribution and embedding in favor of product features
