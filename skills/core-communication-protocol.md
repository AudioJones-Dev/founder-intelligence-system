# CORE Communication Protocol

## Core Idea
Most difficult conversations break because one or both sides feel attacked. CORE is a four-move sequence that keeps the conversation open long enough to reach a real answer.

| Move | Purpose | Sample Move |
|---|---|---|
| **C**uriosity | Signal you don't already know the answer | "I might be missing something — is there context from the last attempt I should know?" |
| **O**bjectivity | Shift focus from people to systems | "Where in the process did this break? Is this a resourcing question or a sequencing question?" |
| **R**eassurance | State your intent explicitly | "I'm raising this because I want us to move faster together, not to assign blame." |
| **E**mpathy | Acknowledge the other side's experience | "I can see how much work went into this, and that the timing wasn't in your control." |

## Why Each Move Matters
- **Curiosity** lowers the threat level before any disagreement is named. It tells the other side they are not yet being judged.
- **Objectivity** moves the disagreement from "you vs me" to "us vs the problem." Systems can be wrong without anyone being bad.
- **Reassurance** answers the question the other side is actually asking: *are you with me or against me?*
- **Empathy** is what makes the other moves land. Without it, curiosity reads as cross-examination and objectivity reads as detachment.

## When To Skip It
- Low-stakes operational conversations. Running CORE on a calendar question is exhausting.
- Emergencies where speed matters more than relational repair.
- Conversations where the other side has already broken trust and the right move is a boundary, not a softening.

## Anti-Patterns
- Running the moves out of order — empathy first can feel like flattery
- Treating CORE as a way to win rather than a way to stay in the room
- Using it on yourself — it's a conversational protocol, not a thinking protocol
