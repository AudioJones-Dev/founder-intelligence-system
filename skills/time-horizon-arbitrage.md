# Time Horizon Arbitrage

## Core Idea
Most operators, markets, and cultures are stuck on the same default horizon — usually the quarter or the year. If your decision's natural horizon is longer than that, you can capture value others cannot, because they will exit, pivot, or sell before the compounding shows up.

The arbitrage is not "be patient." The arbitrage is "match the horizon to the decision."

## Anchor Story
New College, Oxford, was founded in 1379 with a dining hall whose ceiling rested on enormous oak beams. Centuries later the beams began to rot. The college called in the forester, expecting bad news. He told them the original architects had planted a grove of oaks alongside the construction — explicitly grown to replace the beams five centuries later.

The founders made a decision on a 500-year horizon. The arbitrage was structural: anyone trying to source oaks of that size in the panic moment would have failed.

## The Three Horizons

| Horizon | Objective | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 90 days | **Be visible** | What proof of work will land in the next quarter? |
| 12–18 months | **Be valuable** | What asset, skill, or system will compound over the next year and a half? |
| 5+ years | **Be a visionary** | What bet, if it pays, makes the previous two horizons look obvious? |

The mistake is choosing one horizon and ignoring the others. The 90-day work funds the credibility to make the 5-year bet. The 5-year bet gives the 90-day work direction.

## How To Apply
- Before a strategic decision, write down the natural horizon — the shortest period over which its outcome can fairly be judged
- Compare to the horizon the surrounding system will actually use (quarterly review, annual plan, etc.)
- If they don't match, decide which horizon governs the decision *before* the short-term signals start arriving
- Pre-commit to a list of short-term metrics you will not react to

## Anti-Patterns
- Using "I'm playing the long game" to avoid measurable near-term accountability
- Confusing long horizon with no plan
- Failing to plant the oaks — patience without preparation is just waiting
